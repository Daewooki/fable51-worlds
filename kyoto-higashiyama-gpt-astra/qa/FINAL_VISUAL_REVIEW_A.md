## Per-frame findings

- **01 — gion-opening:** Continuous timber rows, inuyarai, utility wire and readable noren establish Gion. Flattened terrain and a populated street termination remove the former exposed slope. The less regular menus/planters and end-wall detail improve the row. The asphalt foreground and similar roofline rhythm remain too uniform.
- **02 — hanamikoji-north:** The south-end garden screen now gives the lane a destination. The right end wall now has a privacy casement and dark rain cladding, while menus/plants are less regular. Similar roof heights and lantern cadence still repeat.
- **03 — hanamikoji-lattice:** Garden gate and blossom now finish the axis. The foreground end gable now has a real projecting privacy casement, meter, cladding and lower window. Its broad planar proportion still dominates about one third of the image.
- **04 — gion-alley:** Good narrow cadence, warm/cool division and restrained red lanterns. The near left gable now has privacy windows and timber cladding, while apron recipes vary. The long repeated facade and roof cadence remains apparent.
- **05 — gion-bicycle:** The bicycle is now visible and correctly separated from the inuyarai. Shop signage and rain chain read at walking distance. The rear context buildings remain simplified and a bare patch beyond the junction still draws attention.
- **06 — shijo-east:** The new stone stair approach makes the Yasaka reveal substantially more recognizable. Deep eaves and shrine/shop material contrast work. Slab edges are rather noisy and the approach remains spatially compressed.
- **07 — yasaka-gate:** The gate is clearly above a broad stair flight. Foreground shop detail supplies useful scale but masks much of the right gate. This is a street reveal rather than a complete gate elevation.
- **08 — yasaka-oblique:** A useful form view with warm vermilion, cool roof undersides and visible bracket sets. The cream shape within the oblique opening initially read as a blockage; the hero builder identified it as a side pier seen through the angled passage. Direct views and the controller walk confirm the opening is traversable. The surrounding sloping apron remains simplified.
- **09 — yasaka-court:** Lantern stage and stone lanterns have strong shrine identity. The foreground omikuji board is still awkwardly cropped and overlapping paving bands create dense aliasing; distant context facades are coarse.
- **10 — yasaka-honden:** Reframing fixes the prior close-up failure: the whole hall and offertory structure can now be judged. The fascia winding correction visibly closes the prior roof/trim sky gap, and bark courses improve material fidelity. The hall still has a large plain side wall and coarse distant context.
- **11 — maruyama-blossom:** A gate, courtyard, stepping path, bamboo and blossom replace the former bare cliff. The place now reads as an authored garden transition. The softer blossom ink reads more cleanly in the final frame. The rear town remains a repeated coarse mass, and green foliage remains visibly faceted.
- **12 — maruyama-garden:** Basin, spout, tree shade, stepping stones and an open gate give the camera a clear garden subject. Duplicate corner slabs have been removed. Moss beds and ground shading remain visibly polygonal, and this is an interpretive pocket court, not a documented Maruyama garden replica.
- **13 — nene-north:** The quieter route is legible and the pagoda is glimpsed above it. The end gables now have privacy casements and lower cladding. Long plaster garden walls and highly repetitive paving still dominate; garden planting remains too sparse in this view.
- **14 — nene-walls:** The lane leads toward a landmark fragment, but duplicate/overlapping paving remains visible at the foreground junction. End-wall windows/cladding improve nearby houses, but the garden walls have little surface/entry variety.
- **15 — ishibe-alley:** Reframed through an opening into a basin garden, with a bicycle and planting. This is a marked improvement over the exposed junction. The large foreground wall and geometric moss shapes remain prominent; it shows a garden glimpse rather than the full winding stone lane.
- **16 — kodaiji-edge:** Bamboo over the wall, shop recesses and slope create useful layered depth. The end gable now has privacy casements, and varied shop work displays are visible beyond. The long plain foreground garden wall still dominates the composition.
- **17 — pagoda-glimpse:** The camera now genuinely reveals the five-tier pagoda framed by shops and blossom. The former absent-landmark failure is fixed. The right end wall now has privacy casements and cladding; the roof fascia gaps are visibly closed. Softer blossom ink separates the landmark from its planted frame. Simplified nearby building proportions and strongly geometric masses still limit the painted-background impression.

## Two bounded improvements implemented and re-reviewed

1. **Articulate exposed machiya end walls.** Add one or two small upper privacy casements with plaster margins, lower rain cladding and a closed service door or meter arrangement. Keep these shallow, use existing materials, and keep shell dimensions/collision unchanged. Target actual failed areas in 03, 04, 13, 14, 16 and 17. A blank party wall can be legitimate, but the same large unarticulated plane should not dominate so many authored views.
2. **Vary shop aprons according to the business.** Replace the mechanical menu/plant sequence with selected pottery crates, tea tins, folded cloth/fans or a low food table, alongside more irregular menu and planter placement. Keep all objects inside the existing apron and preserve an unobstructed route. This directly addresses repetition visible across 01–06 and the hill shops.

# Final visual review A — frames 01–17

Reviewed every unmodified 1600 × 900 image from `qa/final/`, captured 2026-09-04. The capture manifest reports Apple M5 Pro hardware rendering and zero browser errors. This reviewer built the reusable machiya/detail generators, but did not build district placement, terrain, lighting, shrine assets, vegetation or these camera positions. Those parts are independent review; judgments on the shared machiya shell are explicitly not independent self-certification.

**Current verdict: the 17-view subset does not meet the requested minimum 8/10 hero-image bar.** Mean of the subjective category ratings below: **6.70/10**. Scores describe the actual captured result, not implementation effort or the number of generated objects. Frames 10, 11, 12, 15 and 17 especially need camera/context correction. No image in this subset is blank or wholly below the terrain, but that is a much lower standard than finished visual quality.

## Per-frame findings

- **01 — gion-opening:** The continuous timber rows are convincing, but broad featureless asphalt occupies half the composition; distant block shells are still visible.
- **02 — hanamikoji-north:** Recognizable lantern/lattice rhythm. Repeated identical apron props and a bare rising street termination weaken the inhabited-place impression.
- **03 — hanamikoji-lattice:** The intended lattice subject is small while a blank right gable dominates. Raw ground and large low-detail background blocks remain exposed.
- **04 — gion-alley:** Good narrow cadence and warm/cool division. The lane still terminates in a bare slope; menus/lanterns/plants repeat almost mechanically.
- **05 — gion-bicycle:** The bicycle is mostly concealed by or intersects the inuyarai/facade. Large blank background boxes and tan terrain dominate the secondary focal area.
- **06 — shijo-east:** A clear vermilion gate reveal framed by shops. The gate is at street grade rather than atop the recognizable west stair/stone approach.
- **07 — yasaka-gate:** A foreground shop masks much of the right gate and roof. Japanese shop detail is legible; west approach stairs are missing.
- **08 — yasaka-oblique:** The most useful gate form view in this subset. Passage appears backed by a cream wall/terrain mass; needs a verified open visual continuation. Background buildings remain unarticulated.
- **09 — yasaka-court:** Lantern stage and stone lanterns provide strong shrine identity. Foreground omikuji sign is awkwardly cropped; paving intersections show dense overlapping/aliasing lines.
- **10 — yasaka-honden:** Camera is too close: mostly blank slatted wall, a column and cropped offertory box. The shrine hall and precinct cannot be assessed. Reframe several metres back.
- **11 — maruyama-blossom:** The view points into a tall bare tan slope and background boxes; blossom is cropped off the top. It does not show Maruyama garden character.
- **12 — maruyama-garden:** A nearby blank wall occupies roughly the right third. Neither garden planting nor a garden focal point is visible. Reframe into an actual planted area.
- **13 — nene-north:** Coherent wall-and-lane perspective with a pagoda fragment. Large repetitive paving slabs and long nearly bare walls lack Nene-no-michi garden/stone richness.
- **14 — nene-walls:** A quiet corridor, but a raised rectangular paving/curb patch protrudes across the foreground. Repetition and absent garden glimpses weaken the transition.
- **15 — ishibe-alley:** The frame reads as an exposed junction and open ground, not an intimate stone alley. Oversize blank background shells and a large right sidewall dominate.
- **16 — kodaiji-edge:** Bamboo, the low wall and shop frontage create useful layered depth. Blank wall occupies half the frame and the stone surface is still too repetitive.
- **17 — pagoda-glimpse:** No pagoda is visible. This is a pottery shop detail close-up, useful in itself but failing the promised landmark-glimpse viewpoint. Reframe around the facade.

## Cross-cutting findings

The material and lighting system works: cool shadow color is deliberate, warm plaster and timber remain separable, procedural Japanese signs read, and thick bloom/DOF does not conceal the scene. The principal limitations are authored composition and geographic context. The world repeats the same house-apron sequence too consistently, gives important street endings to bare terrain and low-detail shells, and often uses a close-up camera where a district or landmark view is promised. Vegetation is still sparse around the named garden transitions. Those limitations should be described in the delivery report rather than averaged away through generous shader scores.

The captured Yasaka west gate is recognizable, but its nearly level approach omits one of the real location's strongest spatial identifiers: the substantial stair flight, stone terraces and roadside relationship visible in `docs/reference-images/yasaka-west.jpg`. Maruyama and Ishibe-like frames do not yet show sufficiently distinct identities. The town is a compressed procedural interpretation, not a measured replica.

Critical camera/context findings were sent to root immediately for correction. Any replacement image must be re-reviewed; these scores apply only to the files inspected in this pass.

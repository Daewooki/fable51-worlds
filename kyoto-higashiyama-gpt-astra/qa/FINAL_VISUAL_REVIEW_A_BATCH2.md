## Per-frame findings

- **01 — gion-opening:** Continuous timber rows, inuyarai, utility wire and readable noren establish Gion. Flattened terrain and a populated street termination remove the former exposed slope. The asphalt foreground and near-identical shop rhythm remain too uniform.
- **02 — hanamikoji-north:** The south-end garden screen now gives the lane a destination. The right end wall is still a large blank plaster plane; lantern, menu and plant spacing repeats.
- **03 — hanamikoji-lattice:** Garden gate and blossom now finish the axis. The foreground end gable still occupies about one third of the image with very little architectural detail.
- **04 — gion-alley:** Good narrow cadence, warm/cool division and restrained red lanterns. Blank side gables dominate both the near left and distant termination; the same apron recipe recurs.
- **05 — gion-bicycle:** The bicycle is now visible and correctly separated from the inuyarai. Shop signage and rain chain read at walking distance. The rear context buildings remain simplified and a bare patch beyond the junction still draws attention.
- **06 — shijo-east:** The new stone stair approach makes the Yasaka reveal substantially more recognizable. Deep eaves and shrine/shop material contrast work. Slab edges are rather noisy and the approach remains spatially compressed.
- **07 — yasaka-gate:** The gate is clearly above a broad stair flight. Foreground shop detail supplies useful scale but masks much of the right gate. This is a street reveal rather than a complete gate elevation.
- **08 — yasaka-oblique:** A useful form view with warm vermilion, cool roof undersides and visible bracket sets. The cream shape within the oblique opening initially read as a blockage; the hero builder identified it as a side pier seen through the angled passage. Direct views and the controller walk confirm the opening is traversable. The surrounding sloping apron remains simplified.
- **09 — yasaka-court:** Lantern stage and stone lanterns have strong shrine identity. The foreground omikuji board is still awkwardly cropped and overlapping paving bands create dense aliasing; distant context facades are coarse.
- **10 — yasaka-honden:** Reframing fixes the prior close-up failure: the whole hall and offertory structure can now be judged. The hall has plain side walls and a visible gap between the roof perimeter trim and roof surface. The builder traced that gap to reversed fascia winding; the correction awaits this reviewer's image recheck.
- **11 — maruyama-blossom:** A gate, courtyard, stepping path, bamboo and blossom replace the former bare cliff. The place now reads as an authored garden transition. The rear town is still a repeated coarse mass, and foliage clusters are strongly faceted.
- **12 — maruyama-garden:** Basin, spout, tree shade, stepping stones and an open gate give the camera a clear garden subject. Duplicate corner slabs have been removed. Moss beds and ground shading remain visibly polygonal, and this is an interpretive pocket court, not a documented Maruyama garden replica.
- **13 — nene-north:** The quieter route is legible and the pagoda is glimpsed above it. Long plaster walls, large gables and highly repetitive paving dominate; the garden character remains too sparse in this view.
- **14 — nene-walls:** The lane leads toward a landmark fragment, but duplicate/overlapping paving remains visible at the foreground junction and the walls have little surface/entry variety.
- **15 — ishibe-alley:** Reframed through an opening into a basin garden, with a bicycle and planting. This is a marked improvement over the exposed junction. The large foreground wall and geometric moss shapes remain prominent; it shows a garden glimpse rather than the full winding stone lane.
- **16 — kodaiji-edge:** Bamboo over the wall, shop recesses and slope create useful layered depth. A long plain foreground wall and large blank end gable still dominate the composition.
- **17 — pagoda-glimpse:** The camera now genuinely reveals the five-tier pagoda framed by shops and blossom. The former absent-landmark failure is fixed. The right end wall is still extremely plain and the upper roofs show thin bright trim gaps; the hero builder is correcting roof fascia.

## Two bounded improvements selected from this pass

1. **Articulate exposed machiya end walls.** Add one or two small upper privacy casements with plaster margins, lower rain cladding and a closed service door or meter arrangement. Keep these shallow, use existing materials, and keep shell dimensions/collision unchanged. Target actual failed areas in 03, 04, 13, 14, 16 and 17. A blank party wall can be legitimate, but the same large unarticulated plane should not dominate so many authored views.
2. **Vary shop aprons according to the business.** Replace the mechanical menu/plant sequence with selected pottery crates, tea tins, folded cloth/fans or a low food table, alongside more irregular menu and planter placement. Keep all objects inside the existing apron and preserve an unobstructed route. This directly addresses repetition visible across 01–06 and the hill shops.

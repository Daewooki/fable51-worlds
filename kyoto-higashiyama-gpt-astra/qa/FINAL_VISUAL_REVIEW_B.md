### Final per-image observations

- **18 — pagoda-classic:** Strong, clear downhill pagoda reveal. The new lighter bracket details articulate the dark tower, and varied nearby boxes/displays improve apron storytelling. Repeated roof/facade modules remain visible, but this is a successful hero composition.
- **19 — pagoda-close:** Timber fill separates brackets from posts and removes much of the earlier near-black flattening. Upward proportions read, but the portrait has little precinct context and the finial remains largely hidden by the roof. Still more a useful architectural close-up than an exceptional finished landscape.
- **20 — pagoda-reverse:** Warm near shops balance the cool tower; the modeled bracket work is now visible. Side doors/windows improve the previously blank surroundings. The distant uphill shells still appear regular and repetitive.
- **21 — ninenzaka-lower:** Added windows, lower weatherboards and a meter materially improve the opposing side walls. The stepped route is clear and continuous. Large uniform plaster fields and repeated window placement still dominate.
- **22 — ninenzaka-shop:** A stocked incense recess, bench and descending street give useful human-scale detail. No side-wall camera obstruction remains. The caption was subsequently corrected to “Incense on the steps”; the camera and image bytes are unchanged.
- **23 — ninenzaka-steps:** Strong tread perspective and dense near detail; tea cups/stools now make the two shop aprons less mechanical. Side-wall structure and warm/cool separation are clear. This meets the8-level composition/detail bar, with architectural variation still short of9.
- **24 — ninenzaka-reverse:** The downhill bend and roof progression remain persuasive. Removed duplicate apron boards and new side-wall detail improve the view. The continuous repeated shell proportions remain apparent.
- **25 — sannenzaka-lower:** Readable stair climb with fans, ceramics and varied counter displays. Door/counter/stair scale remains coherent. The facade rhythm is dense but still more uniform than real Sannenzaka.
- **26 — sannenzaka-steps:** The new small trays and doorstep objects improve close-range life; side cladding gives the top house a clearer base. Exposed tan ground at the upper turn and repeated upper-floor geometry limit polish.
- **27 — sannenzaka-reverse:** Added lattices and weatherboards resolve much of the former blank near gables. The descending roofscape and lane remain strong. The background context blocks still disclose the procedural layout.
- **28 — kiyomizuzaka:** The open approach, shop clutter and vermilion gate are well composed. Vending machine remains on the right apron. The approach communicates its destination clearly, though shop ordering and frontage repetition do not establish exact site fidelity.
- **29 — kiyomizu-gate:** Saturated vermilion, cream panels, real roof mass and approach stairs read well. New foliage ink is gentler. The very angular cedar silhouettes and sparse ground around the gate remain visibly stylized rather than a highly authored real-precinct reconstruction.
- **30 — kiyomizu-precinct:** The hall plaque, columns and roof underside are readable with painted timber fill. The camera has a coherent veranda position. Opposite-bank shrubs read as isolated pads in places, with large green earth areas still visible; the composition overlaps frame33 heavily.
- **31 — kiyomizu-stage:** The strongest final temple landscape. Timber supports have clear layered structure, roof mass reads, deck trees are gone, and forest frames both sides. The pink masses are quieter and foreground green planting hides much of the raw bank. Lower supports remain more exposed and regular than in the real forested reference, and conifers remain angular.
- **32 — kyoto-overlook:** The raised camera fixes the dominant rail and now gives a clear forest-to-city reward. Pale roof masses and distant hills form an intelligible basin. Foreground vegetation is still composed of repeated canopy types, and the distant city is a simplified band rather than a layered surveyed panorama.
- **33 — kiyomizu-veranda:** Timber fill separates columns, walls and eave brackets while preserving cool shade. Floor/railing geometry reads at human scale. Opposite-bank planting remains sparse and visually detached in places; this repeats much of frame30 rather than adding a distinct landmark revelation.
- **34 — pagoda-postcard:** Clear and unoccluded, with improved bracket detail and no roof gaps. It remains an upward tower portrait closely related to frame19, with limited street context. Useful and recognizable, but not the originally promised classic street postcard.

### Remaining priorities

The remaining visual gap is primarily authoring rather than a missing renderer feature. Street shells need more deliberate height/footprint/eave variation and fewer repeated upper-window arrangements; Ninenzaka/Sannenzaka shop order and corner relationships remain approximate. The veranda-facing hill needs denser continuous undergrowth with fewer isolated foliage pads. The final photo set would benefit from replacing one of each near-duplicate pair19/34 and30/33 with a distinct, geographically grounded arrival/reverse view.

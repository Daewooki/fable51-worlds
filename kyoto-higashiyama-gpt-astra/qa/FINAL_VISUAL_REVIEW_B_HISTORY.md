## Per-image findings

- **18 — pagoda-classic:** The downhill street reveals the five stacked roofs convincingly; storefront depth and small signs frame a recognizably Kyoto composition. The near-right cherry reads as large outlined pads, and the dark violet pagoda loses timber/plaster separation. The large blank upper left wall weakens the otherwise strong frame.
- **19 — pagoda-close:** Strong real eave/bracket geometry and taper. Looking upward gives useful architectural scale, but most timber falls into a nearly uniform dark value. The finial is difficult to read behind the upper roofs. More cool fill or lighter shadow-band response would reveal the modeled detail.
- **20 — pagoda-reverse:** The ascending lane beside the pagoda provides coherent spatial context and dense shop fronts. Pagoda cropping is plausible for a close contextual view, but its near-black surfaces and exposed distant simplified shells reduce polish.
- **21 — ninenzaka-lower:** The narrow stepped climb and frontage rhythm are clear. Two broad, blank foreground side walls dominate more of the frame than the shops. Roof massing and repeated apron arrangements are still more uniform than the photographic references.
- **22 — ninenzaka-shop — BLOCKER:** Roughly two thirds of the image is an unarticulated side wall and retaining wedge. The intended shop is almost edge-on at the left edge. The visible sign is incense rather than the promised pottery subject. Move the camera into the lane opposite an actual display and aim into the recess.
- **23 — ninenzaka-steps:** One of the stronger views: readable merchandise and Japanese signs, layered eaves, exact stone treads and warm/cool separation. The material density is useful at pedestrian height. Repeated display/lantern patterns and uniformly clean stone keep it below the requested exceptional reference fidelity.
- **24 — ninenzaka-reverse:** Strong downhill turn with roofs below the player, convincing thresholds and several shop types. The terrain progression is legible. The neighboring shops remain conspicuously generated from the same apron sequence.
- **25 — sannenzaka-lower:** A clear steep stair climb framed by lanterns, deep eaves and accessible shop fronts. This is a coherent, readable scene with appropriate scale. More uneven frontage, small garden interruptions and varied stair wear would improve correspondence to the real street.
- **26 — sannenzaka-steps:** Good foreground tread structure and uphill destination framing. Counter heights and doorway dimensions read well. Repeated menu boards and facade proportions are the primary limitation.
- **27 — sannenzaka-reverse:** A convincing descending street with long sightline, layered roofs and detailed nearby shops. Simplified distant city geometry becomes visibly regular; foreground side walls remain visually quiet. No terrain clipping is evident.
- **28 — kiyomizuzaka — BLOCKER:** The temple gate reveal and dense approach are useful, but a vending machine sits directly in the road center near world (268,265), interrupting the pilgrimage lane. Move it into a shop apron or recess while maintaining an approachable interaction position.
- **29 — kiyomizu-gate:** The vermilion Nio gate and hip roof are recognizable and better resolved than the earlier self-test. Stone approach and door scale work. The surrounding slopes remain too sparsely planted; obvious stacked conifer cones and bare hills read as a simplified game environment.
- **30 — kiyomizu-precinct — BLOCKER:** Camera is underneath enormous Hondo eaves, facing a mostly blank hall side volume alongside a cropped pagoda. The composition does not present a coherent precinct approach. The combination of hill, supports and wall looks suspended rather than naturally founded. Reframe from an accessible arrival point and review the building-to-earth contact there.
- **31 — kiyomizu-stage — BLOCKER:** The widened valley fixed the earlier below-ground clipping. Real timber supports, veranda and large roof now read as Kiyomizu's defining structure. However two cherry trees are planted on or through the stage/veranda. Remove those placements from the deck or seat them on real ground outside it. The bare high green slope and sparse generic trees expose the entire support grid, whereas the reference is surrounded by dense forest. Structural recognition alone is insufficient for a 9/10 fidelity claim.
- **32 — kyoto-overlook — BLOCKER:** Most of the image is wooden floor, nearby pagoda and hall. Only a tiny pale city sliver is visible, so the promised Kyoto panorama is absent. A stone lane also crosses the wooden stage and terminates abruptly. Move to an accessible railing-facing camera directed west across the basin; remove stone street overlays from the wooden deck/bridge segments.
- **33 — kiyomizu-veranda:** Real columns, roof underside and warm floor shadows offer convincing structural detail. Dark value compression reduces plaster/wood separation. The scene beyond is a bare hill with sparse trees and a tree apparently planted at the deck rail. This is useful hall-detail documentation, but not yet a finished hero landscape.
- **34 — pagoda-postcard — BLOCKER:** Camera intersects or sits behind a storefront frame and noren; around 70% of the image is close dark panels. A narrow gap reveals the pagoda, but this is a camera collision rather than an authored postcard. Move to a clear street position and verify the new result visually.

## Cross-cutting review

The architecture uses real roofs, brackets, lattices, railings, display recesses and timber supports rather than photographs on flat boxes. Canvas signs are legible, and the stepped lanes show a meaningful elevation progression. Depth curvature ink separates geometry without bloom or depth-of-field concealment. These are functional and architectural strengths.

The largest outstanding artistic gap is coherent context: forest around the temple, garden identity between commercial districts, restrained frontage variation and cameras that actually show their named subjects. Pink foliage remains high-key but often reads as separately outlined flattened balls; cedar trees are visibly stacked cones. These are not mature painted canopy masses. Dark pagoda/hall surfaces conceal modeled detail and flatten material separation. The above-average street compositions do not erase the failed temple and postcard frames.

The scene remains a compressed, stylized reconstruction. This screenshot review cannot establish exact building footprints, surveyed terrain, shop ordering or street-level geographic fidelity. Those uncertainties belong in the final delivery report. No 9/10 geographic or style claim is justified by this image set.

All blockers were sent to root and the geographic builder before the report. Subsequent edits require a new capture and review; the scores above apply to the original batch only. Parent-requested low-Gion terrain corrections and Yasaka stair work were performed after this batch and are not credited until recaptured.
